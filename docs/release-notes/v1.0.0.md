Vinyl 1.0.0 is the first public build of Vinyl, a free, open-source menu bar app that puts a 3D turntable on your Mac desktop and follows what's playing in Spotify and Apple Music.

## Highlights

Four scenes, which you can switch between from the menu bar or in Settings:

- **Listening Room**: a desk lit by low afternoon sun through a window. The window frame and a potted plant cast shadows on the wall and desk that shift gently, as if in a breeze. The current album stands on a walnut ledge, the albums you played recently are stacked beside the deck, and a framed Hiroshige print hangs on the wall.
- **After Hours**: the same desk late at night. The light comes from a brass lamp with a glowing glass dome, a warm wash from off-screen, and a glow behind a framed Roesen still life.
- **Turntable**: a top-down view of a brushed-silver deck and the current album's sleeve on a wooden desk, striped by sunlight through blinds.
- **Mini Player**: just the record, with or without its tonearm, in a small floating window.

## Features

- **Spotify and Apple Music without a login.** Both apps broadcast every play, pause and track change to other apps on your Mac, and Vinyl listens for these announcements. Vinyl needs no account, API key or developer setup. **Follow** (Settings › Music) chooses which player Vinyl follows: Automatic (whichever player started playing most recently), Spotify only, or Apple Music only.
- **Optional Connect.** Click **Connect** next to a player in Settings › Music and macOS asks once whether Vinyl may control it. If the player isn't running, Vinyl first opens it in the background so macOS can ask. If you allow it, Vinyl can:
    - show what's already playing when it launches
    - re-sync the tonearm about every 15 seconds while playing
    - use Apple Music's embedded artwork
    - run the play, pause and skip buttons
    - start playback after opening your player when nothing is running

  You can change this later in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Automation.
- **Album artwork.**
    - Spotify covers come from Spotify's public oEmbed service, at 640 px when available.
    - Apple Music uses the artwork embedded in the playing track (this needs Connect).
    - Otherwise, Vinyl searches the iTunes catalogue and only accepts a result whose artist and album match, ignoring case, punctuation and edition tags such as "(Deluxe Edition)".
    - If no cover is found, the sleeve is printed with the album title and artist.

  Covers appear on the record label and the sleeve, and cross-fade when the album changes. The previous cover stays up until the next one has loaded.
- **A tonearm that follows the song.** When playback starts, the arm lifts off its rest and cues onto the record. It moves from the outer groove to the inner groove as the track plays and lifts in place when you pause. The platter turns at 33⅓ rpm, spins up quickly and coasts down slowly.
- **Recently played.** In Listening Room and After Hours, up to five previous albums are stacked beside the deck, with the newest on top. The stack is kept between launches. Until you have a listening history, built-in sleeves fill it.
- **Wide, Mid and Close framing** for every desktop scene. On narrow and portrait displays, the framing widens so the deck still fits.
- **Transparent mode.** Turn off **Room background** and the deck, sleeves and lamp sit straight on your wallpaper, with real shadows.
- **On your desktop.** Desktop scenes sit above the wallpaper and below desktop icons and all windows. They ignore the mouse and appear on every Space. You can show them on the main display or on all displays.
- **Mini player.**
    - Drag it anywhere. It remembers where you put it.
    - Click the record to play or pause.
    - Hover for the playback controls. At Medium and Large sizes, the track name and artist show too.
    - Right-click for playback controls, Picture Disc, tonearm, size and Keep on Top.

  In Settings › Mini Player you can choose a plain label or full picture disc, turn the tonearm on or off, pick Small, Medium or Large, keep it on top, and show it on every Space. The mini player doesn't take focus from the app you're using, and it can run alongside a desktop scene.
- **Menu bar.** The menu bar panel shows what's playing and has playback controls, an on/off switch, the scene picker and quick toggles (framing for desktop scenes, record options for the mini player). Settings opens on first launch, and when you open Vinyl again from Finder or Spotlight while it's running. Closing Settings leaves Vinyl running. The Dock icon only shows while Settings is open, unless you turn on **Show in Dock**.
- **Open at login**, through macOS Login Items.
- **Nothing running?** Press play, or click the record. Vinyl opens Spotify without bringing it to the front and, if it has Automation permission (see Connect), starts playback. It opens Music instead if you follow Apple Music or don't have Spotify.
- **Energy.** On the Automatic setting, Vinyl draws at up to 60 fps on power. On battery or in Low Power Mode it drops to 30 fps, and on Retina displays desktop scenes also render at a lower resolution. Best Quality and Battery Saver use one of these modes whatever the power source.
- **Stops drawing when hidden.** Drawing stops completely when a scene is fully covered, when the display or the Mac sleeps, or when you switch users. While the record isn't spinning, it drops to 20 fps or less. The built-in record counts as spinning, so turn off built-in records if you want Vinyl to idle at that rate when nothing is playing. With Reduce Motion on, the Listening Room's window shadows hold still.
- **Built-in records.** Vinyl comes with five original covers that it generates itself. When no player has a track loaded, the deck slowly plays through one of them. You can turn this off in Settings › Desktop.
- **Private.** No account and no analytics. Vinyl only goes online to look up artwork. It sends the Spotify track ID to Spotify's oEmbed service and, when it needs to search, the artist, album and track name to Apple's iTunes Search API. It then downloads the cover from Spotify's or Apple's image servers. Covers are cached in `~/Library/Caches/io.github.cyclonedraco.vinyl/Artwork`, which keeps the 300 most recently used.

## Install

Download **Vinyl.dmg** below. **Vinyl.zip** has the same app, and **SHA256SUMS.txt** lists checksums (`shasum -a 256 -c SHA256SUMS.txt`).

Vinyl is ad-hoc signed but not notarized by Apple, so macOS blocks the first launch. You need to do this once per version, including after each update.

1. Open `Vinyl.dmg`, drag **Vinyl** into **Applications**, then open Vinyl from **Applications** rather than from the disk image.
2. **macOS 15 Sequoia, 26 Tahoe or later:** when macOS says *"Vinyl" Not Opened*, click **Done**, not Move to Trash. Open **System Settings → Privacy & Security** and scroll down to **Security**. Next to *"Vinyl" was blocked to protect your Mac*, click **Open Anyway**, click **Open Anyway** again in the warning that follows, then enter your password and click **OK**. This needs an administrator, so on a standard account enter an admin's name and password. The button is only there for about an hour after the blocked launch, so if you don't see it, try opening Vinyl again. Right-click → Open no longer gets around the block on these versions.
3. **macOS 14 Sonoma:** in Finder, Control-click (or right-click) **Vinyl** in Applications, choose **Open**, then click **Open**. The **Open Anyway** button in Privacy & Security also works.
4. If macOS says Vinyl "is damaged", don't bypass it. Delete Vinyl and download `Vinyl.dmg` again from this repository's Releases page. If a fresh copy is still blocked and no **Open Anyway** button appears, run this in Terminal and then open Vinyl again. It skips macOS's first-launch malware check, so only do it for a copy from the Releases page.
   ```sh
   xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Vinyl.app
   ```

## Requirements

- macOS 14 Sonoma or later
- An Apple silicon or Intel Mac (universal build)
- Spotify's desktop app, Apple's Music app, or both
- Internet access for artwork lookups (optional; without it, most new albums get a printed sleeve)

## Known limitations

- **Not notarized.** The app is signed ad hoc, not with an Apple Developer ID, so macOS blocks the first launch until you allow it (see Install).
- **No automatic updates.** Check [Releases](https://github.com/CycloneDraco/Vinyl/releases) for new versions.
- **You may need to Connect again after updating.** macOS ties the Automation permission to the app's signature, and every ad hoc build has a different signature.
- **Some features need Automation permission**, which Connect asks for:
    - the play, pause and skip buttons, and clicking the mini player's record
    - starting playback after Vinyl opens your player when nothing is running
    - keeping the tonearm exactly in time
    - Apple Music's embedded artwork
    - showing a track that was already playing when Vinyl launched (without permission, Vinyl picks it up at the next play, pause or track change)
- **Only Spotify's desktop app and Apple's Music app** are followed. Web players and other music apps aren't detected.
- **Artwork matching is strict.** When Vinyl falls back to the iTunes search, it only uses a result whose artist and album match (ignoring case, punctuation and edition tags such as "(Deluxe Edition)"). Anything else gets a printed sleeve, which can happen with local files or rare releases. Albums without a cover aren't added to the recently played stack.
- **Limited automated testing.** The UI smoke test launches a Debug build on an Apple silicon runner that doesn't have Spotify installed, posts simulated Spotify notifications and saves screenshots for manual review. It doesn't fail on visual problems, and it doesn't cover Apple Music, the features that need Automation permission, Intel Macs or the tagged release build.
